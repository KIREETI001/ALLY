# LLM Council — Pressure Test of ALLY v2

## Framed Question

A solo founder in Singapore is rebuilding ALLY as a real venture: an AI caregiver co-pilot whose wedge is turning hospital discharge summaries into executable, multilingual family care plans, whose moat is including the 316,900 migrant domestic workers (MDWs) formally excluded from the government's HealthHub caregiver access, and whose GTM is layered — B2B2C via hospitals/insurers at the discharge moment (primary revenue), B2C freemium with a subsidy navigator as the acquisition magnet, and an MDW-ecosystem layer (agencies, training, referrals) as secondary revenue. Research verified: demand fundamentals are strong; the real competitor is the free government stack (HealthHub AI, SupportGoWhere); regulations permit the product if the parser only transcribes-and-structures (HSA SaMD boundary) and PDPA is engineered seriously; expansion sequence Malaysia → Hong Kong → Taiwan. At stake: the founder's next 12–24 months, ~S$300k of grant-fundable runway, and whether this becomes a company or stays a demo. Is this the right rebuild, and where does it break?

---

## Advisor Responses

### Advisor 1 — The Contrarian
The fatal flaw is hiding in your own spec: **post-discharge care is episodic; your revenue model is recurring.** A family downloads ALLY in crisis week, uses it intensely for 30–60 days, then grandma stabilises or dies — either way the subscription churns. Your wedge and your business model are at war. Second: the B2B2C "primary driver" is a mirage for a solo founder. SG hospital clusters buy through committees, pilots, and procurement frameworks; 12–18 months without revenue is the realistic case, and your $50k Founder grant doesn't survive that. Third: you're betting the moat on government inertia. HealthHub AI already ships four languages on a state budget; adding Tagalog is a content decision, not an engineering one. The Citizens/PR access rule is structural, yes — but Synapxe could ship a "helper view" in one policy memo. Fourth: photo-OCR of discharge summaries sounds clean until you meet reality — crumpled printouts, handwriting, three hospital formats, and a stressed daughter photographing page 2 of 5. Your parse accuracy IS your brand; one hallucinated medication timing and a CNA headline ends you. Fifth: nobody in your plan has validated that families will let a startup read mum's medical documents. Trust deficit vs .gov.sg is real and unpriced. The questions you're avoiding: what's month-13 retention, who signs the first hospital cheque and from which budget line, and what happens when parsing is 94% right?

### Advisor 2 — The First Principles Thinker
Strip the app away. The job being hired: "my mother just got discharged, I'm terrified, I work full-time, the helper doesn't read English — make sure nothing falls through the cracks so she doesn't bounce back to hospital." That job has three irreducible components: translation (medicalese → actions, English → Tagalog), distribution of labour (who does what, today), and reassurance (am I doing this right?). Notice what's NOT in the job: subsidies. Subsidy stress is real but it's a different moment — a financing job, not a care job. Bundling them because both are "caregiver pain" is portfolio thinking, not product thinking; the navigator is a fine magnet precisely because it's a separate, completable task. Now the deeper question: is this a software business at all? The value concentrates in a 60-day transition window — which is exactly what hospital transitional-care programmes (ACTION) are paid for and understaffed to deliver. The first-principles conclusion: ALLY is **transitional-care infrastructure sold per-episode**, not a consumer subscription. Price per discharge episode (S$15–40 institutional, or S$29–49 one-time consumer "transition kit"), and let recurring revenue come from the minority who graduate into chronic-care mode (dementia, stroke, dialysis — multi-year journeys). The founder is asking "how do I make my app better." The right question: "who owns the 60-day post-discharge window in Singapore, and how do I become the rails they run on?"

### Advisor 3 — The Expansionist
Everyone is underpricing the MDW layer. 316,900 in SG, ~360,000 in HK, ~218,000 in TW — that's ~900k care workers across three rich, super-aging markets, ALL structurally excluded from official health-data systems, most working in Tagalog or Bahasa. ALLY's "FDW mode" is not a feature; it's potentially **the operating system for Asia's invisible care workforce**. Follow the threads: (1) Training — CTG money ($400/family) can fund in-app, certificate-bearing micro-courses; agencies need differentiated "care-trained" helpers; you become the credential. (2) Agencies — 1,400+ licensed EAs in SG compete on placement quality; a white-labelled ALLY makes their placements stickier; they become your B2B channel at near-zero CAC. (3) The employer-family-helper triangle is full of latent products: handover documentation when helpers change, structured updates that reduce family-helper friction, eventually insurance products for helper-delivered care quality. (4) The data asset: anonymised, consented care-pattern data (what tasks, what adherence, what outcomes) is exactly what insurers pricing CareShield riders and long-term-care products lack. (5) The navigator can be the "TurboTax of SG eldercare benefits" — July 2026's LTC subsidy enhancement is a once-in-a-decade news hook; own that moment and you own the SEO category. If the discharge wedge works even moderately, the expansion isn't just countries — it's layers. Don't let the regulatory caution in the blueprint shrink the ambition: transcribe-only parsing is the constraint on ONE feature, not on the company.

### Advisor 4 — The Outsider
I just read this cold. Three reactions. First: I don't know what a "discharge summary," "PCHI," or "MDW" is — and neither does the exhausted daughter you're selling to. If your landing page says "AI-powered discharge-to-home transition co-pilot," you've already lost her. She types "mum stroke what now" into Google at 11pm. Meet her there, in those words. Second: I cannot tell, in 30 seconds, why this beats the three free things I already have — the government health app, a family WhatsApp group, and ChatGPT. You know the answer (HealthHub can't make a Tagalog checklist for the helper from a photo of the discharge papers; ChatGPT doesn't know Singapore's subsidies or remind anyone at 8am) — but I had to dig through two documents to find it. That sentence — "photograph the hospital papers, get a checklist your helper can follow in her language" — is your whole pitch. Lead with it everywhere. Third, the trust question: you're asking me to upload my mother's medical records to an app I've never heard of, built by one person. Where's the face? The clinical advisor? The "your data never trains AI" promise in human words? Government apps feel safe because they're boring and official. You need borrowed trust — a hospital logo, a caregiver association endorsement, a real founder story about your own grandmother — or uploads simply won't happen.

### Advisor 5 — The Executor
Monday morning reality check. You cannot sell to hospitals this quarter — you have no traction, no clinical advisor, no case study. Fine. Sequence around it. **Weeks 1–4:** ship exactly three things — parser v2 (photo upload, transcribe-only, provenance), the subsidy wizard with verified June-2026 data, and a WhatsApp-shaped daily digest (even if v1 sends via share-sheet, not WABA — Meta business verification takes weeks, start it NOW). Cut everything else from the sprint, including mood tracking. **Weeks 2–6, in parallel:** incorporate if not already (Startup SG Founder requires the company be under 6 months old — check your ACRA date TODAY, this constraint can silently kill $50k), apply via an Accredited Mentor Partner, and book 20 conversations from CaregiversSG Facebook groups, AIC's caregiver network, and church/CC eldercare groups. Manually onboard every one of them — sit with the discharge papers, watch where parsing fails. **Weeks 6–12:** 20 real families, measure two numbers only — % of discharge documents parsed correctly without edits, and week-4 task-completion retention. Those two numbers ARE your hospital pitch and your grant milestone. **Do not** build the MY locale pack, the agency white-label, or the insurer deck yet. One market, one wedge, twenty families, two numbers. Everything in the blueprint that doesn't serve those two numbers in 90 days is roadmap, not work.

## Peer Review (anonymised A–E)

Solo, full pipeline, PDPA-grade engineering, 20 manual onboardings, grant paperwork — nobody asked whether this needs a co-founder (and several grant programmes effectively do too).

All five missed: **the MDW's own incentive**. Everyone treats the helper as a surface to assign tasks to; nobody asked what makes HER open the app daily — fear of blame? proof-of-work protecting her from accusations? **Reviewer 3:** Strongest: **B** — the 30-second pitch sentence and the borrowed-trust requirement are the most immediately actionable findings here; distribution dies without them regardless of strategy. **Reviewer 5:** Strongest: **E** — it's the only response that answers "what business is this," and its chronic-care graduation path is the retention answer C demanded. Biggest blind spot: **B** — beautiful messaging instincts, but it assumes the daughter is the only buyer; in multi-generational SG households the decision unit is plural (siblings co-pay, helper executes, patient consents), and "one clear sentence" must work in a family group chat, not a landing page. All five missed: **clinical safety review as an asset**. Everyone treats the SaMD boundary as a constraint to stay behind; getting a geriatrician advisor to co-sign the task templates converts compliance into the trust signal B says is missing — one move, two problems.

## Chairman's Verdict

### Where the Council Agrees
The wedge is real and the MDW inclusion is the genuine moat — no advisor disputed the research. The free government stack defines the battlefield: differentiation must be workflow depth and helper inclusion, never generic AI chat. B2B2C cannot be the *first* motion for a solo founder regardless of being the right *eventual* revenue driver — every advisor, from opposite directions, landed on proof-before-procurement. And the 90-day discipline (parser accuracy + retention, ~20 real families) is the convergent next step.

### Where the Council Clashes
**Episodic vs recurring.** The Contrarian says the churn cliff kills the subscription; First Principles says price per episode and let chronic-care graduation carry recurrence; the Expansionist says the layers (training, agencies, data) are the real recurrence. This is the live strategic question, and reasonable advisors disagree because SG has no precedent either way. **Ambition sizing.** Expansionist wants the care-workforce OS narrative funded from day one; Executor wants it deleted from the quarter. Both are right at different altitudes — the failure mode is doing the vision's work on the quarter's calendar.

### Blind Spots the Council Caught
The MDW's own incentive to engage daily (proof-of-work that protects her may be the killer feature nobody specced). Parse-failure UX as the actual trust battleground. Consumer pricing anchored against free — the one-time "transition kit" may outperform subscription. Clinical advisor co-sign as a trust asset, not just compliance. Founder capacity / co-founder question. Calendar-binding constraints: ACRA incorporation age for the $50k grant, Meta WABA verification lead time.

### The Recommendation
Rebuild ALLY as **per-episode transitional-care infrastructure with a chronic-care graduation path** — not a subscription app with a discharge feature. Concretely: keep the blueprint's build order, but (1) price the consumer tier as a one-time Transition Kit (S$29–49) with a chronic-care subscription offered only at week 6 to families whose journey continues; (2) make the MDW's daily proof-of-work view a first-class design object, because her engagement is the moat's load-bearing wall; (3) treat hospitals as a data-collection relationship this year (pilot conversations armed with the two numbers) and a revenue relationship next year; (4) spend none of this quarter on Malaysia, agencies, or insurers — the locale-pack architecture already preserves those options at zero marginal cost. The Expansionist's layers stay in the deck; the Executor's calendar runs the quarter.

### The One Thing to Do First
Within 30 days, put **20 real discharge documents from 20 real families through parser v2** — recruited from caregiver Facebook groups and AIC networks, manually onboarded, failures watched in person — and record the two numbers: % parsed correctly without edits, and week-4 task-completion retention. Everything else — grants, hospitals, pricing — argues from those two numbers.
